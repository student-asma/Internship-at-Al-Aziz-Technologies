Day 5 - Weekly Project & Revision
Overview

This is my Day 5 task for Week 2 of my Data Analytics internship - the final project and revision day. Instead of learning new concepts, this day was about pulling everything I learned across the week (SELECT, filtering, aggregation, GROUP BY, HAVING, CASE, joins, subqueries, CTEs, window functions, date analysis) into one final, business-focused project.

What I Did
Reviewed all four days of the week (Day 1 to Day 4) to make sure the core concepts were clear before building the final project.
Wrote a final SQL file combining the most important analytical queries from the week into one clean, commented script, using the same customers, products, orders, and order_items tables built earlier in the week.
Ran five key analytical queries that focus on answering real business questions rather than just practicing syntax:
Ranking customers by total spending
Total amount spent per customer
Total revenue generated by each product
Monthly sales trend
Customers who have never placed an order
For each query result, I wrote a short business insight explaining what the numbers actually mean, instead of just listing the output.
Put together a documentation report that includes the project overview, the database structure, a summary of what I learned across the week, the query results in table form, the business insights, and a conclusion.
Tools Used
PostgreSQL with pgAdmin 4, using the same database and tables built across Day 1 to Day 4.
Key Business Insights
Usman and Ali are the top two spending customers, while Sara has spent much less in comparison.
The Laptop is by far the biggest revenue driver among all products.
Sales dropped from January to February, which would be worth monitoring over a longer period.
Ayesha is a registered customer who has never placed an order, making her a candidate for re-engagement.

Notes

This day helped me see the bigger picture of why SQL matters for a Data Analyst - it's not just about knowing the syntax for joins or window functions, but about using them together to actually answer a business question and explain what the data is saying.
